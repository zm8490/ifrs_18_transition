## 1. Executive summary: same profit, new shape, new disclosure discipline

1. **Net profit does not change. Its presentation does.** Income and expenses move into operating, investing and financing categories, with two new required subtotals, including a defined operating profit.
2. **Our adjusted measures come inside the audited accounts.** Any adjusted profit we quote to investors or lenders becomes a management-defined performance measure (MPM), reconciled and audited in one note.
3. **The comparative year is already running.** For a 31 December year end, 2026 comparatives must be restated, so account mapping and data capture need to start now.

**We ask the Committee to:**

- Endorse the transition plan and governance
- Agree principles for our adjusted measures
- Mandate a review of loan covenants and incentive targets
- Note the expected change in reported operating profit

**[Assumption]** 31 December year end.

*Speaker notes:* The bottom line is unchanged; this is a presentation standard, not a measurement standard. The governance change is that adjusted measures used publicly move into the audited notes. Because comparatives are restated, the 2026 year we are in now is already in scope.

---

## 2. The one thing to remember

> **Operating profit becomes a defined subtotal. Every ‘adjusted’ profit we quote publicly is audited too.**

That is the core governance change for this Committee.

*Speaker notes:* Today operating profit is not defined in IFRS and each company draws the line differently. Under IFRS 18 it is a required subtotal with defined content. Adjusted measures used in results announcements or investor material must be disclosed and reconciled in a single note, bringing them within the scope of the external audit.

## 3. Scope and timing: mandatory from 2027, with restated 2026 comparatives

| When | What |
|---|---|
| April 2024 | IASB issues IFRS 18, replacing IAS 1 |
| 1 January 2026 | Comparative year begins: data must support restatement |
| 1 January 2027 | Effective date: first IFRS 18 financial year |
| H1 2027 | First interim report uses IFRS 18 headings and subtotals |
| Early 2028 | First annual report under IFRS 18 (FY2027) |

- **Replaces IAS 1.** Most IAS 1 requirements carry over. IAS 7, IAS 33 and IAS 34 are amended alongside.
- **Applied retrospectively.** Comparatives are restated, with a line-by-line reconciliation from the old to the new presentation.
- **Local adoption [Assumption].** We assume our regulator adopts the IASB date unchanged. To confirm: [regulator or listing authority].

If our year end is 30 June, the first IFRS 18 year ends June 2028 and the comparative year began on 1 July 2026. Early adoption is permitted.

## 4. What changes: three changes, one aim — comparable performance

**1 · Structured income statement**
Every income and expense item goes into one of five categories: operating, investing, financing, income taxes and discontinued operations. Two new required subtotals: operating profit, and profit before financing and income taxes.

**2 · Management-defined performance measures**
Adjusted profit measures used in public communication are disclosed in one audited note. Each is reconciled to an IFRS subtotal, with the tax and non-controlling interest effect of every adjustment.

**3 · Grouping and labelling**
New principles on when to combine and when to split items, and less use of ‘other’. If expenses are shown by function, depreciation, amortisation, staff costs, impairments and inventory write-downs are disclosed by nature in a note.

*Also:* the cash flow statement starts from operating profit, and the policy choice on classifying interest and dividends is removed. The balance sheet is largely unchanged.

## 5. The new income statement: five categories and two new subtotals

| Category / subtotal | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Operating** | Main business activities, and anything not classified elsewhere (the default category) | |
| *Operating profit* | | New · required |
| **Investing** | Share of associates and joint ventures; returns on cash and investments | |
| *Profit before financing and income taxes* | | New · required |
| **Financing** | Interest on borrowings and leases; discount unwind on provisions and pensions | |
| *Profit before tax* | | As today |
| **Income taxes** | Current and deferred tax | As today |
| **Discontinued operations** | Under IFRS 5 | As today |
| *Profit for the year* | | Unchanged |

*Speaker notes:* Assumes a non-financial group without a specified main business activity of investing or providing finance. FX differences follow the item that caused them (trade balances stay in operating; FX on borrowings goes to financing). Derivatives generally follow the item they hedge.

## 7. Impact on reported KPIs: what moves, and who will notice

| KPI | Expected effect | Relied on by | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue and gross profit | No change expected | All users | None |
| Operating profit (EBIT) | **Changes:** associates, deposit income and FX on borrowings move out | Analysts, market guidance | Re-baseline and publish a bridge |
| EBITDA and adjusted EBITDA | Becomes an MPM if adjusted beyond depreciation, amortisation and impairment | Lenders, rating agencies | Fix the definition; apply the MPM test |
| Interest cover, net debt to EBITDA | **Inputs may shift** with the new categories | Lenders, covenants | Check facility definitions and any frozen-GAAP clauses |
| Profit for the year and EPS | No change | Shareholders | Adjusted EPS only on an MPM or IFRS subtotal basis |
| Bonus and LTIP targets | **Move** if set on operating profit | Remuneration Committee | Confirm the basis for 2027 targets |

## 8. Management-defined performance measures: our adjusted numbers move into the audited accounts

**Is it an MPM? All three must apply:**

- A subtotal of income and expenses
- Used in public communication outside the financial statements
- Shows management’s view of an aspect of our performance

*Not MPMs:* gross profit, IFRS subtotals, EBITDA defined as operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and IAS 36 impairments, and non-P&L measures such as free cash flow.

**What we disclose, in one audited note:**

- What it measures and why it is useful
- Reconciliation to the closest IFRS subtotal
- Tax and non-controlling interest effect of each adjustment
- Reasons for any change, with restated comparatives

## 9. Notes and cash flow: more granular notes, a tidier cash flow statement

**Aggregation and disaggregation**

- Group alike items; split items that differ
- Avoid ‘other’, or explain what it holds
- Statements summarise, notes give the detail
- Shown by function? Then disclose depreciation, amortisation, staff costs, impairments and inventory write-downs in one note

**Statement of cash flows**

- Indirect method starts from operating profit
- Interest and dividends paid: financing
- Interest and dividends received: investing
- Today’s policy choice goes, so our classification may change

**[Assumption]** We show interest paid in operating cash flows today.

## 10. Systems and data: the work sits in the chart of accounts and consolidation

| Area | Change needed |
|---|---|
| Chart of accounts | Tag every P&L account to an IFRS 18 category; split mixed accounts such as other income and finance costs |
| ERP posting rules | Tag FX gains and losses by the item that caused them; derivatives follow the item they hedge |
| Consolidation and packs | New categories and subtotals in subsidiary packs; equity-accounted results routed to investing |
| Expenses by nature | Capture depreciation, staff costs and impairments for each functional line of the income statement |
| MPM data and controls | Track each adjustment with its tax and NCI effect, under the same controls as statutory numbers |
| Comparatives | Recast 2026 monthly data in the new structure for restated comparatives and the transition reconciliation |

**[Assumption]** One ERP and one consolidation tool across the group. To confirm: [ERP] and [consolidation tool].

## 11. Transition plan (31 December year end): four phases to a clean first IFRS 18 report

| Phase | Timing | Scope | Audit Committee checkpoint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Assess | Dec 2026 – Jan 2027 | Map every P&L account to a category, inventory our MPMs, review covenants and bonus terms | Jan 2027: impact paper and MPM shortlist |
| 2 · Design and build | Feb – May 2027 | Policies and accounting manual, chart of accounts and consolidation changes, restated 2026 figures | May 2027: restated 2026 and MPM policy |
| 3 · First interim | Jun – Aug 2027 | H1 2027 report in IFRS 18 format with the transition reconciliation, auditor review and analyst briefing | Aug 2027: approve first IFRS 18 interim |
| 4 · First annual report | Sep 2027 – Mar 2028 | Year-end controls, full MPM and disaggregation notes, FY2027 annual report | Feb 2028: approve FY2027 accounts |

⚠ If we report quarterly, Q1 2027 is our first IFRS 18 interim, so phases 1 and 2 must finish by March 2027. With a 30 June year end, the whole plan shifts six months later.

## 12. Risks and how we manage them

| Risk | Rating | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Market reads a lower operating profit as weaker performance | High | Early analyst briefing; publish a bridge from old to new subtotal |
| Covenant or bonus definitions drift from the new subtotals | High | Legal review of facility agreements; Remuneration Committee decision on bonus basis |
| 2026 data not captured in the new categories | Medium | Start account mapping now; recast monthly results |
| MPM disclosures challenged by auditors or the regulator | Medium | Written MPM policy, controls and early auditor alignment |
| Finance team capacity during the 2026 year end | Medium | Phased plan; external support for mapping and recasting |
| Local adoption date or requirements differ from the IASB | Low | Monitor the regulator; plan to the IASB date |
